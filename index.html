Featured Research

Co-first author 2024 Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology

Finding the active compounds in a traditional medicine for rheumatoid arthritis

We screened a classic Chinese herbal formula, Juan-Bi decoction, for compounds that could treat rheumatoid arthritis. One compound, bornyl acetate, reduced joint damage in mice.

  • 15active compounds identified
  • 2experimental models (cells + mice)
  • 1validated anti-arthritic compound

My contribution

  • Formal analysis of large network and pathway datasets
  • Data visualizations in R and Excel
  • Review and editing of the manuscript for clarity and accuracy
Read the study summary

Juan-Bi decoction (JBD) has been used to treat rheumatoid arthritis (RA) for centuries, but its active ingredients weren't known. We filtered its compounds by ADMET and physicochemical properties and predicted their targets. Then we built a compound–target–pathogenic gene network to rank each compound's effect on RA genes. We tested the top candidates in LPS-induced RAW 264.7 cells and a collagen-induced arthritis mouse model.

Bornyl acetate reduced inflammatory cell infiltration and kept the cartilage surface smoother. It appears to work by suppressing steroid hormone biosynthesis.

Co-first author 2019 American Journal of Translational Research

Finding a key driver of two aggressive lymphomas

We combined 10 public datasets and found that the gene E2F8 is a likely biomarker and drug target in Burkitt's and mantle cell lymphoma. This was its first reported link to these cancers.

  • 10datasets integrated
  • 1,448patients analyzed
  • 3lymphoma types compared

Skills used

  • Merging and harmonizing data across studies
  • Differential expression and pathway analysis
  • Regulatory network analysis and visualization
Read the study summary

The analysis covered 352 Burkitt's lymphoma, 880 diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and 216 mantle cell lymphoma patients, plus 33 controls. We compared the biological pathways affected in each type and mapped transcriptional regulatory networks. E2F8 had the strongest effect in Burkitt's and mantle cell lymphoma, activating genes involved in the cell cycle, mitosis and immune function.
